# What the PP1 reference-extension study does and does not answer

Reviews: **ws3J 6/Strong Accept (conf 3) · swx9 3/Borderline reject (conf 5) · yNv5 3/Borderline reject (conf 3)**.

The new study is a 3 × 4 grid — three reference sources (**A real photographs**,
B GPT-Image, C Qwen-Image) × four generators (nano_banana, MOSAIC, **Flux.2-klein-9B**,
**GPT-Image-1.5**) over 216 prompts, with **22 subjects that have zero overlap with
MIB's 80**, plus two fresh human-annotation batches (**2592 votes, 8 annotators, every item
rated by exactly two**).

It lands squarely on three review points, including the most damaging one. It does
not touch several of swx9's. Both halves are below.

---

## Answered — strongly

### 1. yNv5 W3: "A single training matchup … evidence for unseen generalization is limited"

> *All 60K training pairs are Nano Banana vs. MOSAIC. MIE scores 0.982 on seen
> (= these two) but drops ~0.10 to 0.884 on unseen. With training this narrow, the
> evidence that unseen generalization extends to a broader range of generator
> families is limited.*

This is the objection the new data was built for. The `pp2` batch is
**Flux.2-klein-9B vs GPT-Image-1.5 — neither generator appears anywhere in MIE's
training**, and they come from different families than the training pair (a
step-distilled flow model and a closed API model, vs Flux.1+LoRA and Gemini).

| | value |
|---|---|
| AUC (MIE Δtotal → human preference) | **0.815** [0.755, 0.868] |
| existence correlation, GPT-Image-1.5 | **r = +0.942** (annotator κ 0.924) |
| existence correlation, Flux.2 | r = +0.528 (annotator κ 0.808) |
| agreement by subject count (2/4/6/8) | 88.4 / 85.0 / 89.8 / **92.4 %** |

Two things to say beyond the headline. First, alignment **does not decay with
subject count** — it is marginally best at 8 subjects, which is the regime the
benchmark exists for and where MIE's training data is thinnest. Second, MIE's
*margin* is calibrated: agreement runs 82.5 % → 98.5 % → 100 % across
|Δtotal| < 0.5 / 0.5–1.5 / > 1.5, monotonically, on two independent batches. A
predictor that had merely memorised the training matchup could not do that on two
unseen generators.

### 2. swx9 W3 + Q6, and yNv5 W2: "references are GPT-Image generations … synthetic"

> *swx9: the subject references have been generated by GPT-Image. This is not ideal,
> as real faces are harder to generate than synthetic faces.* / *What was the
> reasoning behind using synthetic subjects?*
>
> *yNv5: Reference subjects are GPT-Image-1 generations … the reference identity is
> synthetic and may not be self-consistent, injecting hard-to-bound noise into the
> Appearance dimension.*

The study re-runs the identical 216 prompts against three reference sources, one of
which is **real photographs** (licensed stock, 22 subjects, downsampled to 512).
Results on the unseen-generator batch:

| reference source | n | AUC | agreement | existence r (GPT-Image-1.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — real photographs** | 173 | **0.827** | 91.9 % | **+0.936** |
| B — GPT-Image | 176 | 0.851 | 91.5 % | +0.935 |
| C — Qwen-Image | 181 | 0.775 | 84.0 % | +0.968 |

**Real references perform as well as synthetic ones** — AUC 0.827 vs 0.851, all three
CIs clear of 0.5, and the highest per-facet correlations split across A and C. MIE's human
alignment does not depend on the references being synthetic and self-consistent, so
the noise channel yNv5 worried about is not what is carrying the result.

(On `pp1` the A and C AUCs are undefined: annotators were unanimous, so there is no
negative class. Agreement there is 99.5 % / 99.0 % / 98.5 % across A/B/C.)

### 3. ws3J Q2: "does MIE extend to unseen classes?"

**The 22 PP1 subjects have zero overlap with MIB's 80** — verified by slug
comparison, not assumed:

| category | subjects |
|---|---|
| human (10) | black_teen_girl_braids, east_asian_man_leather_jacket, elderly_black_woman_headwrap, elderly_south_asian_man, latino_man_black_polo, man_orange_turtleneck, middle_eastern_teen_boy, middleaged_white_woman_glasses, southeast_asian_woman, woman_pink_hair |
| animal (5) | alpaca, dairy_cow, flamingo, hamster, turtle |
| object (5) | desk_globe, electric_kettle, picnic_basket, shopping_cart, violin |

So the numbers in §1–2 are simultaneously unseen-generator **and** unseen-subject.
Flamingo / alpaca / desk_globe also start to reach the longer tail ws3J asked about,
though 22 subjects is not a long-tail study and we should not call it one.

## One number we must not put in the rebuttal

MIE agrees with the human majority on 89.1 % (pp2) and 99.0 % (pp1) of items. Both
batches are heavily one-sided, so a predictor that **always names the majority
winner** scores 92.3 % and 99.8 % — it beats MIE on both.

| batch | MIE | always-majority | MIE − baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| pp1 | 99.0 % | 99.8 % | **−0.8 pp** |
| pp2 | 89.1 % | 92.3 % | **−3.2 pp** |

Quoting the agreement rate invites a one-line refutation from a reviewer at
confidence 5. Everything above is stated in AUC (a constant predictor is pinned at
0.500) and in per-facet correlation (a constant predictor cannot produce one at all)
for exactly this reason.

## Limitations to state before a reviewer finds them

1. **MIE over-credits Flux.2 existence**: 0.829 against humans' 0.562 — a +0.267 gap,
   and the only existence correlation that drops (0.528 vs 0.85–0.94 for the other
   three). MIE is lenient generally: 8 of 12 facet biases are positive.
2. **The appearance head cannot be validated by these annotations** — annotator
   κ is 0.19–0.34 on appearance, i.e. the humans do not agree with each other, so no
   correlation is achievable. This is a limit of the annotation protocol, and worth
   saying plainly: it does not confirm the appearance head and it does not condemn it.
   It also partly *concedes* yNv5 W2 — appearance is the noisiest dimension.
3. **Neither batch is a close call** (pp2 is 92/8, pp1 99.8/0.2). 112 of pp2's 648
   items are additionally dropped because the two annotators split. The highest-value follow-up is a
   pair humans split nearer 50/50; by MIE's own scores `gpt15 vs nano_banana` is the
   closest candidate (62/38, consistent across all three reference sources).
4. `A/flux2` is missing 6 of 216 images, all in the hardest `occlusion_interaction`
   class — so that one cell is very slightly biased toward easier prompts.

## Suggested framing

Lead the rebuttal to **yNv5** with §1 and §2: the narrow-training-matchup objection
and the synthetic-reference objection are the two that drove the 3s, and this study
answers both with fresh human labels on unseen generators, unseen subjects, and real
photographic references. Lead the reply to **ws3J** with §4 (the scorecard they asked
for) and §3 (unseen classes). For **swx9**, be upfront that this study addresses the
reference-synthesis concern and nothing else on their list, and treat the
distribution table and the Hungarian-ID comparison as separate commitments — swx9 is
at confidence 5 and will check.
