\begin{abstract}
Binding is the central failure mode of multi-subject personalized generation. Once multiple reference identities must co-exist in the same image, current text-to-image models often omit subjects, entangle appearances, or assign interactions to the wrong entities. Yet existing evaluators, largely calibrated for single-subject alignment or global aesthetics, are poorly equipped to detect these failures and often lose ranking separability as subject count increases. To address this gap, we introduce \textbf{BindBench}, a systematically controlled benchmark for multi-subject personalized generation. BindBench is built on hierarchical prompt construction over 36 controlled buckets spanning relation type, human/object ratio, and scene complexity ($N \in \{2,4,6,8\}$). To jointly support scalable metric training and rigorous evaluation, we construct a decoupled data regime consisting of (1) a \textbf{60K-pair Silver Set} generated by Nano and Mosaic and annotated by dual teacher VLMs (Gemini 2.5 Flash and Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite), and (2) a \textbf{4K-pair Gold Human Evaluation Set} covering six diverse generators, including unseen systems such as Flux and GLM, via double-blind human annotation. To demonstrate the benchmark's utility, we provide \textbf{AnchorScore}, a strong reference-conditioned evaluator baseline trained exclusively on the silver set with a dual-head ranking-and-diagnosis objective. AnchorScore exhibits strong zero-shot cross-generator generalization on the gold set, showing that large-scale silver supervision can serve as a practical training bed for automated evaluation. By open-sourcing the 60K resource, BindBench offers the community a benchmark not only for measuring metric failure, but also for developing stronger evaluators and preference data for future generative alignment.
\end{abstract}
